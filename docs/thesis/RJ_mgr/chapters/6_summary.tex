\chapter{Summary}\label{sec:conclusion}

The aim of this thesis, stated in Section~\ref{sec:aim}, was to design a transparent multi-agent decision support system for stock trading on the Warsaw Stock Exchange and to evaluate it under a protocol rigorous enough that its results can be trusted. Both parts of that aim were met, and the second turned out to matter more than the first.

The system itself is deliberately simple: five signal agents, each confined to one methodological family, feed a decision agent that combines their signals into a linear, confidence-weighted score and thresholds it into a trading action. The evaluation around it is not simple. It combines anchored walk-forward tuning, calibrated commissions and slippage, five risk-adjusted metrics, a volatility-matched comparison, bootstrap confidence intervals and paired tests, a decomposition into market regimes, a leave-one-agent-out ablation, sensitivity analyses over the family weights and the selection objective, a cost-sensitivity sweep, an audit of the confidence heuristic, two machine-learning baselines, and a full replication on a second market.

What that evaluation established is narrower than what the project set out to find, and more defensible. The ensemble is not a return enhancer. Passive buy-and-hold earned more on both test windows, because both were dominated by bull regimes and a partially invested strategy cannot keep pace with a fully invested one in such conditions. The ensemble is, however, a risk reducer, and consistently so: it halved the maximum drawdown on WIG20, cut the integrated drawdown by nearly two-thirds, led on every risk-adjusted ratio reported, and overtook the benchmark on total return once both series were placed on the same ex-ante risk budget. Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses} stated three hypotheses; the evidence is consistent with all three on point estimates, and with none of them at conventional levels of statistical significance on a single window of under two years.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

The work supports the following conclusions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item the multi-agent system improves risk-adjusted performance rather than gross return, leading buy-and-hold on Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar, Ulcer, and Martin measures while trailing it on cumulative profit in bull-dominated windows,
    \item the advantage is concentrated where the design predicts it should be, namely \CorrectionDefencePP{} percentage points of correction-regime defence and \SidewaysExcessPP{} percentage points of sideways-regime carry, paid for by underperformance in the bull phase,
    \item the profile is not an artefact of any single design choice, since it survives the removal of any one agent, perturbation and re-estimation of the family weights, every plausible cost calibration, a change of selection objective, and a three-fold chronological re-split,
    \item the profile is not specific to the Warsaw Stock Exchange, since the FTSE~100 replication reproduces it under a different constituent set, currency, and cost regime that includes an asymmetric buy-side transaction tax,
    \item the transparent ensemble remains competitive with an LSTM and a gradient-boosted-trees classifier evaluated under the identical protocol, both of which achieve their apparent edge on the FTSE window by remaining near-fully invested through a bull market,
    \item the confidence heuristic does not work as designed, since confidence values carry no measurable information about signal-level accuracy on either market, so the inverse-variance reading of the aggregation rule is a conceptual analogy rather than a calibrated mechanism,
    \item the mechanism that does operate is error diversity, since pooling signals that correlate weakly or negatively across the methodological families lowers the variance of the aggregate position however the pool is weighted,
    \item none of the observed advantages reaches conventional statistical significance on a single sub-two-year test window, and they are therefore reported as economically meaningful rather than as statistically established.
\end{enumerate}

The sixth and seventh conclusions are the ones that were not anticipated at the outset, and they are worth stating plainly. The architecture was motivated by the idea that a confidence value could act as a proxy for the reliability of an agent at a given moment, so that the decision agent would perform something close to precision-weighted averaging. The confidence-validation experiment of Section~\ref{subsec:confvalid} shows that this is not what happens. The system works on the risk axis, but for the weaker and more robust of the two reasons that forecast-combination theory offers.

Methodologically, the evaluation pipeline is the more transferable contribution. It closes the four gaps identified in Chapter~\ref{sec:related_work} and transfers as a template to comparable studies on smaller equity markets, where preserving capital through turbulent periods may count for more than pursuing the next bull leg.

\section{Difficulties and limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The study carries several limitations.

First, the two markets evaluated are both blue-chip indices of large capitalisation, sampled at daily frequency. Extending the finding to thinner and less liquid stocks, to other asset classes or to intraday data would demand a separate evaluation, and agents built around daily closes would need adapting before any intraday use.

Second, the cost model covers commissions and slippage and is stress-tested across a wide grid, but liquidity constraints, partial fills and the price impact of larger orders all lie outside it. Sizeable orders would move the closing price and would call for simulation at the level of the order book.

Third, nothing beyond price enters the system. Fundamental, news and alternative sources are known to carry information orthogonal to price-based indicators, and each would sit naturally in the forecast-combination framework as a further agent with its own error structure.

Fourth, the decision agent runs on fixed family weights and heuristic confidence values. Section~\ref{subsec:weights} finds the headline conclusions robust to perturbing, reordering and re-estimating those weights, and finds in-sample optimisation no reliable improvement on the fixed prior; the weights nonetheless remain static over time, and regime-conditional weighting is untested. Section~\ref{subsec:confvalid} finds the confidence values uninformative about signal-level accuracy, so no calibrated precision weighting can be claimed and the link to classical forecast-combination theory stays conceptual.

Fifth, the test window reaches across bull, correction and sideways regimes but remains a single window of under two years, on which the bootstrap carries limited power against moderate effect sizes. Point estimates and confidence intervals consequently bear as much of the argument here as $p$-values do.

Sixth, matching the two series on volatility calls for leverage of $\lambda = \Leverage{}$, which lies beyond the reach of a typical retail investor on the WSE. That comparison is a counterfactual on risk budget and not an operational recommendation.

Finally, no live-trading exercise was undertaken, and any claim about realised performance in production would require one.

Two difficulties beyond these shaped the course of the work. One was computational. Running the full battery means re-running the backtest hundreds of times over grid points, ablations, weight perturbations and cost configurations, which stayed intractable until signal computation was separated from portfolio simulation and the agent signals precomputed once (Algorithm~\ref{alg:wf}). Runtime then fell by better than an order of magnitude, and the sensitivity analyses became affordable.

The other was interpretive. An early version judged the system against buy-and-hold on cumulative return alone, and by that measure it looked like a failure. Seeing that the comparison was confounded, since a partially invested strategy was being set against a fully invested benchmark running at roughly twice the volatility, is what prompted the risk-adjusted battery, the regime decomposition and the volatility-matched comparison that now sit at the core of the evaluation.

\section{The way forward}\label{sec:way_forward}

Each limitation points to a direction for further work.

The FTSE~100 validation invites extension to further indices, the German DAX, the Italian FTSE MIB or the US S\&P~500 among them, and to smaller-capitalisation and emerging-market segments, which together would map more precisely the conditions under which the risk-adjusted advantage holds. Moving to intraday frequencies would require revising the signal agents and the transaction-cost model together, and would expose the system to market-microstructure effects it currently ignores \cite{tusien2025mining}.

Fundamental and alternative data would enter as further signal agents behind the same base-agent interface, which the modular design of Chapter~\ref{chap:implementation} makes straightforward.

Since neither static in-sample optimisation nor inverse-variance estimation adds much (Section~\ref{subsec:weights}), the promising weighting extension is a regime-conditional one, raising the trend family through bull regimes and lowering it through corrections. That follows naturally from the present fixed-weight design and should recover part of the bull-regime shortfall that Section~\ref{subsec:regime} identifies.

A complementary direction replaces the heuristic confidence values with calibrated probabilities, mapping indicator magnitudes onto empirical hit rates estimated on the training window by an isotonic or Platt-type fit \cite{murphywinkler1987}. Genuine precision weighting would then become available to the decision layer, and the conceptual link to forecast-combination theory would turn into a quantitative one open to test. Given what Section~\ref{subsec:confvalid} found, this is the single most valuable extension of the present design.

The roster itself could be made adaptive rather than fixed, its active pool selected per regime or per symbol from recent forecast accuracy, so that an agent contributes only where it has demonstrated skill. More ambitiously still, a learning-based ensembling layer, a stacked meta-learner or a gating network fitted to the data, could displace the linear confidence-weighted score. The difficulty there is to admit a richer and possibly non-linear combiner without surrendering the per-agent attribution and interpretability that motivate the present design.

Finally, a walk-forward exercise against a paper-trading broker interface would close the gap between backtest and realistic execution, and would put to the test the operational assumptions the backtest takes for granted, chiefly end-of-day fills at closing prices without market impact.
